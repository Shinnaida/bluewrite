READING THE INCIDENT'S OWN DETAILS

You already read this account once and named the kind of incident it is. You are
now reading the SAME account again for the fields THAT KIND of incident carries:
the vehicles and their plates, the items taken, the injuries, the entry point.

THE ACCOUNT IS THE ONLY SOURCE, exactly as before. It is the officer's own
words, in whatever order, in English, Bisaya (Cebuano), Tagalog, or any mix. It
is the same text you already read; nothing has been added to it.

ONE RULE GOVERNS EVERY FIELD:

  Report what the account STATES. Leave everything else EMPTY. An empty field is
  a correct, expected answer and is how you tell the officer "your account does
  not say this". An invented value is worse than an empty one, because the
  officer may not notice it before it is filed.

  Which means, specifically:
   - Never invent a plate number, a name, an amount, a quantity, a date or a
     time. If it is not in the account, it is empty.
   - Never convert, total, re-format or "correct" a value the account gives.
     Copy names, plate numbers, streets and amounts EXACTLY as written.
   - Never answer a field from the general shape of the incident ("a hit and run
     normally means the driver fled" is not something the account said).
   - A field with a controlled vocabulary accepts ONLY the values listed for it,
     spelled exactly as they are spelled there, and only when the account
     supports one of them. If the account supports none of them, the field is
     empty. Never answer such a field with a synonym or a word of your own, and
     never with the account's prose: an injury described as "bruises on her left
     arm" belongs in the free-text fields, while the coded field that asks for a
     status takes the listed     value the account supports ("Minor Injury"), or
     nothing. In a row, "nothing" means the field is LEFT OUT of the row — never
     the closest-sounding value: nobody said the suspect was hurt, so no row says
     he was uninjured. The vocabularies are listed under
     `fields_that_take_one_of_these_values` — for the incident's own fields in
     the block below, and for a group's fields inside that group's entry.

  HOW FIELDS ARE NOT AN EXCEPTION TO THIS. A field that asks how, why or in what
  manner is answered by the account's own words about the act itself — "grab her sling bag
  from behind and run" answers How property was taken (grabbed from behind, a
  snatch); "the padlock was cut" answers it too; "a motorcycle came out from the
  side and hit my tricycle" answers how the collision happened. Writing the
  account's OWN fact in the field's words is not inventing, and it is expected:
  the account rarely phrases things the way a form does. What stays forbidden is
  adding a fact the account does not give. So a how/why field the account
  describes must never come back empty, and must never be listed in `unstated`.

THE GROUPS (people and things, one entry each)

  Under `groups` you answer with ARRAYS. Each entry is one person or one thing
  the account names — one vehicle, one stolen item, one injured person — and
  every field of that entry comes from the account's own words about THAT
  vehicle, item or person. Never carry a fact from one entry into another, and
  never add an entry the account does not name: an empty array is the correct
  answer when the account names none.

  Roles are already settled (a victim is a victim, a suspect is a suspect) —
  this call is about the DETAILS of the people and things already named, and
  about any further person the account names by name.

  A THING THE ACCOUNT DESCRIBES IS A THING THE ACCOUNT NAMED, even when it is
  described inside another thing's sentence. Accounts talk the way people talk:
  "a sling bag containing her cell phone, her wallet with about P3,000 and some
  of her IDs", "the drawer held a pistol and two magazines", "a red motorcycle
  with a box on the back hit my tricycle". Each named thing is recorded — the
  container AND each thing it held, the motorcycle with whatever the account
  says about it — with only the fields the account gives for it (a quantity, an
  amount or a plate only when the account states one). The officer can merge or
  delete a row in a second; they cannot recover an item you left out. The
  reverse is just as wrong: never add a row for a thing the account does not
  name.

WHAT YOU COULD NOT READ

  Under `unstated`, list in plain English the fields the account leaves out that
  an officer would normally need for this kind of incident — one short line each,
  no advice, no guess at the answer ("no plate number given", "no injury
  described"). This is what the officer is shown so they can add it themselves;
  it is never written into the report.

  A fact the account gives in PROSE is not unstated. If the account says what a
  bag held, the contents are recorded above and are NOT "unstated"; if it
  describes the road, the weather or the direction of escape, those are not
  unstated either. One line each, and only for what the account genuinely does
  not say.

ANSWER with the JSON object the schema describes. Fields you cannot answer are
empty strings, groups you cannot answer are empty arrays, and nothing else
belongs in the answer.
